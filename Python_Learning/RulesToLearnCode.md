# 12 Rules to Learn to Code

### 1. Trick Your Brain with the 20min Rule

- The more regularly you code, the more likely it i sthat you'll have ripped coding muscles.
- It is not easy for our brain to do task switching, so try doing 20mins a day, and you will be absorbed without even realizing
- Develop a habit and let your body get used to it

### 2. Code for a Purpose

- Start the process with the purpose in mind. When you are just mindlessly doing it, you will never finish it because you don't have a purpose.

### 3. There's is no "perfect language to learn"

- Everyone will have different opinion on which tools are the best. But just because they think it i sthe best, it doesn't mean that it is something that you need.
- Remember to only focus on picking the best tool for the task

### 4. Understand what you're writing

- Remember that when you write the code, ensure that you also understand what you are writing. When someone just give you a code, remember to check it.
- The key is ramping. You want to be stretched over and over again and for knowledge to be built on previous knowledge. If that ramp is too steep, you'll get lost, and if its too shallow, you'll get bored.

### 5. It's Ok to Not Know

- Most engineers have Imposter Syndrome, meaning that just because you think you don't know osmehting, deosn't mean that you are bad at it.
- The skill you need to home is in asking good questions and understanding the answer.

### 6. Be a Copycat

- In the beginning, it is best to make something. BUt when you don't know what to make, copy it. Make your own apps that copy others. It is okay to copy others idea when learning.

### 7. Be accountable to someone. Show your work

- Show someone your work. Internal motivation is not strong in any of us. YOu need accountability and commitment to learning.

### 8. Keep Learning

- The world will keep moving, if you stay in one place, you'll eventually be left behind.

### 9. Play Foosball

- Sometimes people tend to complain taht proogrammers are always doing something else that doesn't look like work. But they are looking for bugs
- This is salways a painful experience, but it is worth the learning

### 10. Get a mentor - Try Pair Programming

- Good mentors don't solve your probelms, rather they practice the socratic method of asking good questions that get you to think for yourself.

### 11. Get into the Habit of Chunking

- Break each module down even further, and think about alternate ways of solving the problem in the process.

### 12. Break someone else's code

- When troubleshooting answer from Stackoverflow, break the solution code, line-by-line, and understand what each line does and why it's been written.
